## 1. How judging works

Sites are scored by jurors (industry professionals) and the community. As publicly documented, the weighted split for the jury is roughly **Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20%, Content 10%**. Scores run 1-10; the daily top-scored site (above a threshold, and after jury vote windows close) becomes **Site of the Day**. Scores in the mid-6s and above earn **Honorable Mention**; roughly 7+ territory is needed for SOTD, with the winner usually in the high 7s or 8s. Sites are also scored on mobile separately in the "Mobile" badge track.

Jurors view on desktop, often on large screens and decent connections, and they open dozens of sites a day. **First 5-10 seconds matter**, but they also scroll deeply and test interaction, resize, and check mobile.

## 2. The four criteria

### Design (40%)
What jurors look for: a unique visual language; strong typography; cohesive color; composition and layout; imagery quality; attention to detail; consistency across pages.
Build for it:
- A defined art direction with one big idea, expressed in type, color, grid, imagery.
- Typographic hierarchy with real contrast and a distinctive display face.
- Custom imagery or considered treatment; no stock feel.
- Detail work: alignments, hairlines, hover states, loading states, 404, forms.
Red flags: template layouts, inconsistent spacing, weak type pairing, clip-art illustration, default UI components.

### Usability (30%)
What jurors look for: clear navigation, orientation, speed, responsive behavior, accessibility, intuitive interactions, error handling.
Build for it:
- Obvious wayfinding (current section, back, menu), no mystery meat.
- Load fast; LCP under ~2s on realistic connections; no layout shift.
- Native-feeling scroll and keyboard support; visible focus states.
- Mobile layouts designed, not shrunk. Touch targets 44px+.
- Reduced-motion and contrast support.
Red flags: scroll-jacking, hidden menus, janky animations, broken mobile, long forced preloaders, unlabeled icon buttons.

### Creativity (20%)
What jurors look for: originality, innovation in concept, interaction, storytelling, or technology; "I haven't seen this before".
Build for it:
- One novel interaction or narrative device tied to the content (not generic).
- Surprising but legible metaphors (navigation as a map, scroll as time, cursor as a lens).
- Technical craft that enables a new feeling (shader transitions, physics, audio-reactive visuals with opt-in).
Red flags: trend-chasing without concept, copying a recent SOTD's signature effect, effects unrelated to the message.

### Content (10%)
What jurors look for: quality and relevance of copy, imagery, video, structure; storytelling; usefulness; tone.
Build for it:
- Specific, voiceful copy; real case studies with outcomes.
- Strong media (photography, video, 3D) edited and consistent.
- Information architecture that serves the story.
Red flags: lorem ipsum, vague claims, empty sections, padding.

## 3. SOTD vs Honorable Mention

| Dimension | Honorable Mention | Site of the Day |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | Competent, polished | Singular idea, memorable |
| Design | Clean, on-trend | Distinct visual language, authored details |
| Motion | Nice reveals | Choreographed, purposeful, signature moment |
| Performance | Decent | Smooth on mid devices, fast load, no jank |
| Usability | Works well | Effortless, accessible, considered edge cases |
| Content | Good copy | Strong storytelling and media |
| Mobile | Responsive | Designed experience, equal craft |
| Risk | Safe | Takes a point of view; executes it flawlessly |

SOTD winners rarely have one weak axis. A brilliant concept with poor usability or performance gets pulled down; a flawless but generic site stalls at Honorable Mention.

## 4. Common reasons sites miss

1. Looks like other recent winners (derivative scroll effects, same fonts, same grid).
2. Great hero, weak inner pages.
3. Heavy loading or stutter on scroll (especially Safari and mid-range laptops).
4. Mobile treated as a fallback.
5. Content thin or generic.
6. Navigation confusion, hidden back/close, broken deep links.
7. Bugs at unusual viewport sizes or after resize.
8. Over-animation: everything moves, nothing leads.

## 5. Submission prep

- Test on Chrome, Safari, Firefox; Windows and macOS; at least one iOS and one Android device; ultrawide and 13" laptop.
- Prepare: concise project description (what, why, how), technologies, credits, and a clean URL (no staging banners, no cookie walls over the hero).
- Ensure titles, meta, OG images, favicon, and 404 are complete.
- Record a clean screen capture or provide stable links for key interactions.
- Launch the site fully and wait for stable analytics and no console errors.
- Submit when the site is polished at launch quality; resubmissions are hard to sell.
- Consider also: Developer Award criteria (technical excellence), CSSDA and FWA as secondary targets.
